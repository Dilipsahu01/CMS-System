Yes. For your CMS, I would define **three access levels clearly**:

1. **Public / Visitor** — no account required
2. **Registered User** — account required
3. **Admin / CMS Staff** — privileged CMS access

And then put AI **inside the CMS editor as an AI Website Builder/Assistant**, rather than making AI a separate disconnected feature.

Here is the architecture I would give your coding agent.

```text

                           │             │
                        EDITOR          ADMIN

                           │
                     Limited CMS
```

A more production-oriented RBAC model:

```text
ROLE
│
├── Public
│
├── User
│
├── Author
│
├── Editor
│
└── Admin
```


# 10. AI SAFETY / PERMISSIONS

Do **not** allow AI to bypass CMS permissions.

The AI should operate with the same permissions as the logged-in user.

```text
USER
 │
 ▼
AUTHENTICATION
 │
 ▼
ROLE / PERMISSION CHECK
 │
 ▼
AI
 │
 ▼
CMS ACTION
```

For example:

```text
Author
  │
  └── AI can create draft
              │
              └── ❌ AI cannot publish if author lacks publish permission
```

Admin:

```text
Admin
  │
  └── AI can create/edit/publish
```

But even for admins, I recommend:

```text
AI generates change
       ↓
Preview
       ↓
Human approval
       ↓
Publish
```

rather than:

```text
AI → automatically publish everything
```

### My recommendation for your roadmap

Don't implement the full AI website generator immediately. Do it in this order:

**Phase 1:** Secure the three access levels + proper RBAC.

**Phase 2:** Finish the block-based website builder.

**Phase 3:** Add **AI block generation** — `"Add a testimonials section"`.

**Phase 4:** Add **AI content editing** — rewrite, translate, SEO, summarize.

**Phase 5:** Add **AI full-page generation** — `"Create a college website with Home, About, Events and Contact"`.

**Phase 6:** Add **AI conversational website builder** — the user describes what they want, AI builds/changes the CMS structure, shows a preview, and waits for approval.

That last phase is the feature I'd use to differentiate your CMS from simply being a **Payload CMS + Next.js implementation**.
